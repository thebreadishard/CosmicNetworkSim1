# Related work: models of civilizations spreading through the galaxy

Notes on existing models and simulations, collected 25 October 2026. Purpose: know the field, and be precise about what this simulator adds.

## Existing models

- **Hart (1975), Jones (1976–1981), Newman & Sagan (1981)** – Classic Fermi-paradox work. Mostly analytical or simple Monte Carlo models of how fast a single civilization could colonize the Milky Way with spacecraft. Typical outcome: a few million years, short compared with the age of the galaxy.
- **Bjørk (2007)** – Simulates exploration of the Milky Way by self-replicating space probes. *Exploring the Galaxy using space probes*, International Journal of Astrobiology. https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/international-journal-of-astrobiology/article/exploring-the-galaxy-using-space-probes/5A4484AD26B9B876FCDE70B85D63BE19
- **Forgan (2009 and later)** – Monte Carlo "numerical testbed" of the galaxy that tracks, star by star, whether life and civilizations arise and disappear; used to test hypotheses about extraterrestrial life and intelligence.
- **Carroll-Nellenback, Frank, Wright & Scharf (2019)** – Settlement simulation in which civilizations colonize stars while the stars themselves move through the galaxy. Conclusion: the galaxy can fill up in waves, so it is not strange that we have not (yet) been visited. *The Fermi Paradox and the Aurora Effect: Exo-civilization Settlement, Expansion and Steady States*, AJ. https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.04450 — popular summary: https://www.quantamagazine.org/galaxy-simulations-offer-a-new-solution-to-the-fermi-paradox-20190307/
- **Hanson et al. (2021), "grabby aliens"** – Model of civilizations that expand across the whole universe until they meet; used to explain why humanity appears "early". *A simple model of grabby aliens*. https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.01522

Large cosmological simulations (of dark matter, gas, stars and galaxies) do not include life at all.

## What this project adds

The models above are almost all about **spread**: who reaches which star, and how fast. They do not model what happens once planets with life become **connected**: which new functions emerge in such a network, and how the network changes the chances of survival. That network of life is the focus of this simulator.
